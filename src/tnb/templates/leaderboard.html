therapy-note-bench

LLM-written psychotherapy session notes, scored on two published protocols — TN-Eval's SOAP rubric and iCARE's 17 sections — and re-run as models change. Code and data on GitHub.

What this leaderboard can and cannot tell you → Four claims, four figures, and the file each number came from — for somebody building or buying one of these. Also as a PDF.

How this was measured → Every column defined, which judge and how closely it agrees with two therapists, how far the two judges disagree with each other, what the two corpora are and where they came from, and which rows are no longer drawn.

Every column's range, what it counts and what it must not be read as are printed under the table it belongs to, not down here — including the near-zero agreement between two therapists on Faithfulness (Krippendorff's alpha 0.18).

Notes counts the notes a protocol could actually read. A model that writes a good note in the wrong shape loses notes to its format, not to its clinical content — read the count before comparing the score.

The last-but-one column is the provider that served the model. The same model id on two providers can be two different builds — a different quantisation, different weights, a different system prompt — so they are two rows here, never one.

Rows are only ever compared when they agree on all six of track, harness version, prompt version, judge model, judge prompt version and the settings the judge ran at — which is why the judge is a switch above and not a column here. What has been withdrawn from the tables, and why, is on the methods page.

What the corpora are, and what they are not. Four pages carry the background these tables rest on, and until 2026-08-27 nothing on this site linked to any of them: the datasets — where each came from, what licence it publishes (two of the three publish none), and the traps in them; the method; what a result cannot claim; and what exists in this field and what does not.
