__STYLE__

therapy-note-bench

LLM-written psychotherapy session notes, scored on two published protocols — TN-Eval's SOAP rubric and iCARE's 17 sections — and re-run as models change. Source and method.

How this was measured → Which judge and how far it agrees with two therapists, how much the two judges disagree with each other, what the corpora are, and which rows are no longer drawn.

The eight-page briefing → What a leaderboard of these models can and cannot tell you, for somebody building or buying one — four claims, four figures, and the file each number came from. Also as a PDF.

Every column's range, what it counts and what it must not be read as are printed under the table it belongs to, not down here — including the near-zero agreement between two therapists on Faithfulness (Krippendorff's alpha 0.18).

Notes counts the notes a protocol could actually read. A model that writes a good note in the wrong shape loses notes to its format, not to its clinical content — read the count before comparing the score.

The chip after a model name is the provider that served it. The same model id on two providers can be two different builds — a different quantisation, different weights, a different system prompt — so they are two rows here, never one.

Rows are only ever compared when they agree on all six of track, harness version, prompt version, judge model, judge prompt version and the settings the judge ran at — which is why the judge is a switch above and not a column here. What has been withdrawn from the tables, and why, is on the methods page.
