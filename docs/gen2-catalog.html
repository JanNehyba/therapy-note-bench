K01
Support of claims in the source

Definition

Every factual claim in the note has support in the session transcript or in another declared source. Lack of support divides into invented (the source has nothing of the kind) and distorted (the source says something else).

Rule (method claims)

The note is decomposed into atomic claims. Each claim carries its actor (who said or did it), its time (when, and whether it is a change of state) and its modality (fact, hypothesis, plan, agreement, degree of certainty). Each claim is verified against the transcript (P04). A claim that has support in the transcript at a different actor, time or modality is contradicted, and the error is typed as actor, time or modality (P05).

Outcome: supported
the transcript carries the claim with the same actor, time and modality
Outcome: contradicted
the transcript carries the opposite, or the same content at a different actor, time or modality; the error type is recorded
Outcome: unsupported
the transcript carries nothing that supports or contradicts the claim
Outcome: unassessable
the claim cannot be decided from the transcript; recorded as such

One method shared by K01, K02, K03, K04.

K05
Coverage of essential content weighted by importance

Definition

The share of essential information from the session that the note carries, where essentiality is graded and derived from the source and the purpose, not from the existence of a field in a form.

Rule (method qa)

Before any note is read, a bank of information units is built from the transcript. Each unit is one fact that a continuation of care or a documentation audit would need, and carries an importance level (mandatory, mandatory in certain circumstances, highly recommended, good to have, optional), a purpose tag (continuity, audit), a type tag (plan, risk, other) and the transcript span it rests on. For each unit the answerer receives the note only and states whether the note carries it. The answer is compared with the bank. Coverage is reported per importance level and per purpose, never as one sum (P01); a unit the note reports as not asked or as explicitly denied is answered with that status, not as omitted (P02); risk-tagged units are reported as their own layer that no other unit can dilute (P03).

Outcome: preserved
the note carries the unit with the same content, actor and time
Outcome: partial
the note carries part of the unit, or with an unclear referent or time
Outcome: omitted
the note does not carry the unit and does not say that it is missing
Outcome: contradicted
the note states the opposite of the unit, reports as not asked what the transcript asked, or reports as denied what the transcript affirmed
Outcome: unassessable
the answerer cannot decide from the note; recorded as such, never as omitted

One method shared by K05, K06, K07, K08, K10.

K09
Justification of created relations

Definition

If the note connects pieces of information into a relation, a link or a conclusion, that relation is grounded in the source and appropriate to the purpose.

Rule (M15)

A relation is anything the note asserts between two things it also reports: a cause, a trigger, a consequence, a progression, a diagnosis drawn from symptoms, an effect attributed to an intervention. The rater locates each one and answers two questions in order. First, does the transcript support both parts and the link between them? Second, where the parts are supported and the link is the note's own inference, is drawing it here what the note is for? The second question has no external standard and the answer is the rater's; that is why agreement between raters is the only evidence this item has.

Question
For each relation the note draws, is it carried by the session, or is it the note's own inference?
Answer: carried
the session itself makes the link; the note reports it
Answer: inferred_warranted
the session does not make the link, the note draws it, and the rater judges that a clinician writing this note would draw it too
Answer: inferred_overreaching
the parts are in the session, the link is the note's, and the rater judges it goes beyond what this session shows
Answer: unsupported
a part of the relation is not in the session at all, which is also an unsupported claim under K01
Answer: not_applicable
the note draws no relation; it reports without connecting
Example: carried
The client says the argument with her sister is why she stopped going out; the note records that.
Example: inferred_overreaching
The client mentions an argument and, separately, poor sleep; the note writes that the argument caused the insomnia.
Example: boundary
The note writes that the client responded to the reframe by softening. The session shows the softening after the reframe but not because of it; whether that is warranted is the rater's call and the disagreement is the finding.
Rater
therapist; the same pass that verifies claims, because the relations are found there

Why it stands here

The supported half of the construct is in P04; the purpose-appropriateness half needs a purpose-specific standard that does not exist yet.

K30

Definition

The note reads as a clinician of that language would write it: ordinary clinical register, no machine phrasing, no idiom carried over from another language. Distinct from comprehensibility: a stilted note can be perfectly understandable.

Rule (M10)

A clinician who writes notes in that language rates how the note reads as a piece of writing in it, with the content set aside. Four steps, and the rater is told that a stilted note can be perfectly understandable and a fluent one can be wrong: this item is about the writing, not about the facts.

Answer: stilted

Answer: translated

Definition

Definition

The note shows that the client's cultural, linguistic and social context was taken into account where it bore on the session: how the client's background is recorded, whether an adaptation or an interpreter is noted, and that no assumption about the client stands in for what was said. K33

Definition

K34

Definition

A clinician reads the note against the transcript for its terms. Families, an organising aid

F1 Fidelity to the source and the status of information

Whether a statement in the note matches what was said in the session, and whether its origin, time, certainty and speaker are preserved.

Boundary. Boundary with F3: an unsupported interpretation is a fidelity failure, but a justified interpretation grounded in the source belongs to synthesis. One wrong statement can be found by QA and by claim verification; those are not two independent errors.

Evidence: S1, S2, S10

F2 Coverage of essential content

Whether the note carries what is genuinely essential for the given recipient and purpose, not whether it filled every field.

Boundary. Boundary with F6: a field required by a specification is a specification criterion, not evidence that the content is clinically essential. Omission and an explicit negative finding must not be conflated.

Evidence: S1, S2, S3

F3 Processing and clinical synthesis

Whether the note builds relations between pieces of information justifiably and to the degree the purpose requires, without being rewarded for inventing a clinical story.

Boundary. Overlaps with F1: every synthesis is also a statement whose support can be verified. Kept separate because missing synthesis is not the same as false synthesis.

Evidence: S2, S3

F4 Comprehensibility, organisation and economy

Whether the note can be read and navigated, and whether every part of it serves something.

Boundary. The shortest note is not the best one. Sentence relevance, redundancy, level of detail and bare length must be kept distinguishable; PDSQI measures them with one attribute, TN-Eval in another way.

Evidence: S1, S2

F5 Respect, privacy and safety of sharing

Whether the note speaks about a person in a way that holds up when they read it, and whether it carries sensitive detail its purpose does not need.

Boundary. The requirements here depend most on jurisdiction and recipient and are least supported by direct research on psychotherapy notes. iCARE asks for contact details; that contradicts data minimisation rather than confirming it.

Evidence: S2, S3, S13, S14

F6 Traceability and conformance to the specification

Whether a statement can be traced to the source and whether the note meets a stated, declared specification.

Boundary. Validity of a citation, its semantic support and the coverage of claims by citations are three different things. Meeting a decree is not evidence of quality, and the reverse.

Evidence: S2, S14, S1

F7 Usability in the task

Whether the note actually carries the task it was written for: taking over care, supervising, understanding as a client, passing an audit.

Boundary. These are outcome indicators, not properties of the text. They are measured on people doing the task; a number from an LLM is a stand-in here, not evidence.

Evidence: S6, S7, S1

K39 Error inherited from the source

The note faithfully renders something the source itself got wrong. In this benchmark the source of the Czech track is speech recognition output, so a mis-recognised word copied correctly into the note is wrong about the session and right about the transcript. It is the one property in the catalog that cannot be decided from the transcript, because the transcript is what is wrong.
