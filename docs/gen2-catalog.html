Families, an organising aid

F1 Fidelity to the source and the status of information

Whether a statement in the note matches what was said in the session, and whether its origin, time, certainty and speaker are preserved.

Boundary. Boundary with F3: an unsupported interpretation is a fidelity failure, but a justified interpretation grounded in the source belongs to synthesis. One wrong statement can be found by QA and by claim verification; those are not two independent errors.

Evidence: S1, S2, S10

F2 Coverage of essential content

Whether the note carries what is genuinely essential for the given recipient and purpose, not whether it filled every field.

Boundary. Boundary with F6: a field required by a specification is a specification criterion, not evidence that the content is clinically essential. Omission and an explicit negative finding must not be conflated.

Evidence: S1, S2, S3

F3 Processing and clinical synthesis

Whether the note builds relations between pieces of information justifiably and to the degree the purpose requires, without being rewarded for inventing a clinical story.

Boundary. Overlaps with F1: every synthesis is also a statement whose support can be verified. Kept separate because missing synthesis is not the same as false synthesis.

Evidence: S2, S3

F4 Comprehensibility, organisation and economy

Whether the note can be read and navigated, and whether every part of it serves something.

Boundary. The shortest note is not the best one. Sentence relevance, redundancy, level of detail and bare length must be kept distinguishable; PDSQI measures them with one attribute, TN-Eval in another way.

Evidence: S1, S2

F5 Respect, privacy and safety of sharing

Whether the note speaks about a person in a way that holds up when they read it, and whether it carries sensitive detail its purpose does not need.

Boundary. The requirements here depend most on jurisdiction and recipient and are least supported by direct research on psychotherapy notes. iCARE asks for contact details; that contradicts data minimisation rather than confirming it.

Evidence: S2, S3, S13, S14

F6 Traceability and conformance to the specification

Whether a statement can be traced to the source and whether the note meets a stated, declared specification.

Boundary. Validity of a citation, its semantic support and the coverage of claims by citations are three different things. Meeting a decree is not evidence of quality, and the reverse.

Evidence: S2, S14, S1

F7 Usability in the task

Whether the note actually carries the task it was written for: taking over care, supervising, understanding as a client, passing an audit.

Boundary. These are outcome indicators, not properties of the text. They are measured on people doing the task; a number from an LLM is a stand-in here, not evidence.

Evidence: S6, S7, S1

K01 Support of claims in the source

family: F1 kind: construct origin: prior_local state: proposed continuity: high supervision: high client: high audit: high

Every factual claim in the note has support in the session transcript or in another declared source. Lack of support divides into invented (the source has nothing of the kind) and distorted (the source says something else).

Origin
Existed in the project as Factual agreement, from the reproduction of the instrument. The review now adds the verbatim three-way typology from TN-Eval and the fabrication/falsification distinction from PDSQI-9. Finding a source does not change where the idea came from.
State
proposed. P04.
Response mode
categorical per claim (4 outcomes)
Unit
atomic claim (actor, time, polarity, certainty)
Inputs
session transcript, note, segmentation into claims
Applicability
Always. Unavailable context or a failed retrieval makes a claim unassessable; that is not the same as false.
Positive example
Transcript: the client says they sleep about four hours. Note: The client reports sleeping about four hours.
Negative example
Note: The client reports insomnia since the divorce. No divorce was mentioned in the session.
Boundary example
Note: The client seemed tired. The transcript only contains a yawn; a therapist's observation can be neither verified nor refuted from a text transcript.
Measurement
Verification of claims against the source with four outcomes: supported, contradicted, unsupported, unassessable.
If it fails
An invented fact propagates into further care and the client reads it as something said about them.
Validation
Direct evidence from S1 for English and public MI demonstrations. No Czech validation.
Overlaps
K02-K04; one wrong claim is one finding, not four.
